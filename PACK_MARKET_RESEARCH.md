# Pack Market Research — What $99–$300 AI/dev products actually deliver

**Status:** Internal research doc (not public marketing). Feeds the Agent
Operations Pack definition and gates the relaunch. Author: product-manager
agent, 2026-07-13. All prices/claims are cited; where a figure is approximate
it's labeled.

**Bottom line up front:** A code-only, clone-and-go product sells for $150–$250
with *zero video* and thousands of buyers (ShipFast). Video is common in the
tier but **not required** for a developer audience when the deliverable is
runnable. Our unfakeable asset — a real in-production AI-run company with
documented failures and real dispatch history — maps exactly onto what the
market rewards at premium prices. **Recommendation: a runnable scaffold +
real role-brief templates + annotated real history is a clear $99 yes; add
1–3 short "proof" screencasts of the actual system, not a full video course.**

## 1. Deliverable breakdown by real product

| Product | Price | What's in it | Video? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ShipFast** (Marc Louvion) — Next.js SaaS boilerplate | **$150–$250** | Clone-and-go code: auth, Stripe payments, emails, DB, blog, UI, SEO. 8,300+ buyers; testimonials cite shipping in ~7 days. | **No video course** — the code *is* the product |
| **ZTM AI Agents Bootcamp** | **$399 one-time** (or $25/mo all-access) | Video lessons, real-world projects (e.g. AI Interview Coach), CrewAI/LangGraph/MCP, live community classroom, 30-day refund | Yes (video-led) |
| **AI Hero — "Claude Code for Real Engineers"** (Matt Pocock) | **~$795** cohort | 8 workshop modules, live office hours, Discord, lifetime access, certificate | Yes + live |
| **Josh Comeau** — CSS-for-JS / Joy of React | **~$150–$400** | Blended: text + video + **interactive playgrounds** + coding challenges + "boss" projects | Yes, but interactivity is the signature, not video alone |
| **Boot.dev** — Build an AI Agent in Python | subscription | Hands-on, code-first, in-browser | Mixed |
| **Microsoft "AI Agents for Beginners"** | **FREE** | 18 lessons + full GitHub repo | Free baseline |
| **"Hands-On AI Agent Mastery"** (indie) | subscription | 30-day code-first, GitHub repo, ReAct/RAG/multi-agent | Text/code-led |

**Which components correlate with the $99+ tier vs sub-$50:**
- **$99+ correlates with:** a runnable artifact you *keep* (boilerplate/repo/templates), a specific do-this-after outcome, lifetime access, and/or live/community access. Depth and "I now have a working thing" drive the price.
- **Sub-$50 (Udemy-tier) correlates with:** video hours as the headline unit ("40 hours!"), broad intro coverage, no durable artifact, commodity positioning.
- **Key inversion:** at the high end, *hours of video is not the value unit* — the durable deliverable is. ShipFast charges more than most video courses with no video at all.

## 2. Video specifically — is it table stakes?

**No — video is common but not required at $99–$300 for developers.** The
decisive evidence is ShipFast: a **$150–$250 code-only** product with thousands
of buyers and no video course [ShipFast]. Boilerplates/templates as a category
routinely sell $99–$250 on the strength of runnable code, not footage.

Where video appears (ZTM $399, AI Hero $795), it's paired with *live* elements
and higher prices — video is a lever for the $300+ course-shaped tier, not a
floor for a $99 developer *tool/asset*.

On format, when video is used for this audience, **screencasts / code-alongs**
(Wes Bos style) outperform talking-head, and Josh Comeau's own view is that
**interactivity beats passive video** — a draggable/runnable thing teaches
better than a clip [Comeau/badass.dev]. For us that's important: *runnable
code is a superior substitute for video*, not a lesser one.

**Implication:** we do not need a video course to justify $99. A runnable
product clears the bar. Short screencasts are a conversion *booster*, not a
prerequisite (see §4).

## 3. What justifies the price / what makes it feel thin

**Worth-it drivers (from reviews/positioning):**
- **A concrete "what you can DO after"** outcome, not "feel confident." Buyers
  who can name the capability they gained rate courses worth-it [DigitalDefynd].
- **Immediately applicable / practical**, revisitable with **lifetime access**
  [Uxcel/Udemy review].
- **A durable artifact** you keep and adapt (the boilerplate pattern).
- **Proven, credible instructor** — trust in the source [Medium/Darroman].
- **Fair, simple refund policy** (30–60 day) signals confidence.

**Thin / refund drivers (the #1 risk):**
- **~30% of online-course buyers regret a purchase**; refunds are among the
  most common e-learning complaints [DigitalDefynd].
- **#1 driver: "I could get this free / it's generic."** For agents
  specifically, a *free* Microsoft repo of 18 lessons is one search away — a
  paid product that's just "intro to agents" is dead on arrival [BrightCoding/Microsoft].
- **Misleading marketing** — promised more than delivered — is the top trust
  breaker and refund cause [Trustpilot/ConsumerAffairs]. (Note: this is
  literally our historical failure mode; the Pack must under-promise.)
- No clear outcome; padding hours to look substantial.

**Translation for us:** to be a clear yes, the Pack must (a) deliver a durable
runnable artifact, (b) name the exact outcome ("your own agent fleet running"),
(c) be visibly *not* reproducible from the free course/repo, and (d) never
imply contents that aren't there.

## 4. Our angle — monetizing an unfakeable asset

Our differentiator is the one thing none of the comps have: **a real,
in-production AI-run company** — an orchestrated fleet (CEO + specialists +
independent review gates), a documented *catastrophic failure and recovery*,
and real dispatch/PR history. The build-in-public literature says exactly why
this is premium teaching value:
- "A founder/lead engineer who explains trade-offs, publishes design docs, and
  **walks through failures earns credibility ads can't buy**" [dev.to/Trust-by-Design].
- "**Production systems are defined by separation of responsibilities**, not
  feature count — that's what separates engineers who plateau from those who
  grow" [Medium/Kumar]. Our CEO/specialist/review-gate structure *is* that
  lesson, made concrete.

**Where we have an edge:**
- Real role-brief prompts + a runnable scaffold = the ShipFast model applied to
  agent orchestration (a category with no dominant boilerplate yet).
- The failure catalog is unique, credible, and impossible to fake — it directly
  answers the "why pay vs the free repo" objection.

**Where we have a gap:**
- **No video today.** But per §2 this is not fatal, and our real system turns
  the gap into an asset: short **screen recordings of the actual fleet running
  a real dispatch + review cycle** are *proof footage no competitor can make* —
  low production cost, high credibility, and they double as onboarding.

## 5. Recommendation — the deliverable stack for a clear $99 yes

Model the Pack on **ShipFast (durable runnable artifact), not on a video
course.** Stack, in priority order:

1. **Runnable clone-and-go scaffold** — the spine. Zero → working multi-agent
   fleet on the buyer's own repo. This alone clears the $99 bar (ShipFast
   proves code-only sells at $150+). *Non-negotiable.*
2. **Real role-brief templates** (all 6 seats) — the unfakeable IP; the
   "separation of responsibilities" lesson as adaptable files.
3. **Annotated real dispatch/PR history + failure→pre-flight checklist** — the
   credibility moat that beats the free Microsoft repo. PII-scrubbed.
4. **1–3 short "proof" screencasts (10–20 min total) of the REAL system** —
   NOT a video course. Real footage of a real AI company running a dispatch and
   a REQUEST-CHANGES review. Converts the no-video gap into a unique asset at
   low cost. *Recommended, not required for v1.*
5. **Refund-proofing:** a one-line "what you can DO after" outcome, lifetime
   access, living updates, and a simple 30-day refund.

**On video, explicitly: NO full video course for v1.** It's the wrong value
unit for this audience and this asset, and it's the biggest production cost.
Do the runnable artifact first; add short real-system proof clips as the only
video. Revisit a fuller video track only if buyers ask.

**Price read:** at this stack, **$99 is comfortably justified and $149 is
defensible once the scaffold + screencasts land** — consistent with the comps
(ShipFast $150–250 code-only; ZTM $399 with video+community). The clear-yes
hinges on the *runnable scaffold shipping*, exactly as flagged in the Pack
definition; a docs-only bundle at $99 would hit the "thin/generic" refund
driver head-on.
